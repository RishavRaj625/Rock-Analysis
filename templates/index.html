Main Mineral Classifications:

Silicates

Silicate minerals contain the silicon-oxygen tetrahedra ([SiO₄]⁴⁻) as their fundamental structure, comprising over 90% of Earth's crust. Examples: Quartz, Feldspar, Mica, Talc.

Carbonates & Oxides

Carbonates feature the anionic complex [CO₃]²⁻ (e.g. Calcite), reacting to weak acids. Oxides comprise metal cations bound to oxygen (e.g. Hematite), serving as essential metallic ores.

Sulfides & Sulfates

Sulfides are compounds of sulfur combined with metal cations (e.g. Pyrite). Sulfates contain the anionic group [SO₄]²⁻ (e.g. Gypsum), typically forming as evaporites in dry lake beds.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

What types of mineral samples can be analyzed? ▶
The platform is engineered to identify rock-forming minerals, focusing on silicates, carbonates, oxides, sulfides, and sulfates. It supports eight core classes: Quartz, Feldspar, Calcite, Hematite, Mica, Pyrite, Gypsum, and Talc.
How accurate is the deep learning mineral classification model? ▶
Our custom CNN classifier is trained on an augmented high-resolution mineral database, reaching a validation accuracy of over 95%. Prediction confidence scores are provided with each scan to indicate system classification certainty.
What visual analytics does the platform provide? ▶
The platform provides detailed visual overlays (OpenCV edge scans, Canny edge superimposed overlays, crack contours, K-Means color distribution, Grad-CAM heatmaps, and SHAP saliency grids), alongside 3D crystal system models, Leaflet deposit maps, and training stats charts.
Can I export the geological analysis reports? ▶
Yes! Upon completing an image analysis, you can click "Download PDF Report" to download a complete document generated dynamically with ReportLab, containing all properties tables, chromatographic curves, K-means pie charts, and morphological/XAI overlays.
How is the Mineral Quality Index (MQI) computed? ▶
The Mineral Quality Index (MQI) is a composite score calculated using a weighted formula: 40% model inference confidence, 30% OpenCV assessed image quality metrics (combining brightness, contrast, and sharpness parameters), and 30% sample purity derived from the surface roughness and crack density indexes.
What is represented by the Plexus constellation background? ▶
The interactive plexus constellation network background represents the microscopic atomic crystal lattice structures and chemical bonds that characterize minerals. The occasional bright white node flares mimic crystalline light reflection and energy flares within minerals.
What are the Explainable AI (XAI) overlays (Grad-CAM and SHAP)? ▶
Grad-CAM (Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping) highlights features at the final convolutional layers that dictated the predicted mineral class (e.g. highlighting grain patterns). SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) attribution shows pixel-level contributions, allowing researchers to trace exact neural pathways of classification.
How does the interactive 3D crystal system viewer work? ▶
Based on the predicted mineral, the platform references its mathematical crystallography system (e.g., Cubic for Pyrite, Hexagonal/Trigonal for Quartz, Monoclinic for Gypsum). It then generates a corresponding 3D crystal model in real-time using WebGL via Three.js, complete with wireframe sub-lacing for interactive structural inspections.
